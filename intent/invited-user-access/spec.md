# Google-authenticated invitations and user access

**Accepted by the owner for local testing · 2026-11-26.** Based on the [accepted intent](intent.md) at `528dfa3`. Approval includes the pilot ban threshold and the ten-minute acceptance window per emailed invitation. Production remains owner-only, with invitations and independent labeling on hold.

## Requirements

1. **Two ways in.** A visitor types their own address on the interest form, signs in with Google and confirms submission. The owner can also invite a chosen recipient directly. Typing an address alone must never send mail, create a review item, or count against the address holder.
2. **Screen before preview.** System 1 decides whether a verified interest request may enter the owner's queue. Only eligible requests appear. The owner can **Approve** or **Deny**. Approval permits a separate **Send invite** action. A denial sends no mail. Only the sole owner may review, send, revoke, or lift a ban.
3. **Redemption before access.** Every non-owner signs in with Google and redeems a valid invitation for that account before entering the workspace. Acceptance must complete within 10 minutes of sending that recipient's email. Every such member receives the user role. No request, spreadsheet cell, classifier decision or delivery receipt conveys membership.
4. **Suppress repeats and ban repeated offenders.** A retry, double click, pending invitation or repeated expression of genuine interest is a duplicate. Count abuse separately, attribute it to the verified actor, and provide expiry and appeal. An attacker must not be able to ban someone by typing their email.
5. **Private, durable tracking.** Maintain one authoritative record for each action and an owner-only Google Sheets reporting copy. Preserve decisions across restarts and concurrent Cloud Run instances. Keep addresses, tokens and applicant activity outside the public intro, shared presence and guardrail panels.

## Design

### What the visitor and owner see

| Step | Expected behavior |
|---|---|
| **Request access** | A fixed link on the public intro opens a separate admission service. Email entry and Google verification happen there. Before verification, keep the typed value only in a short-lived, protected session. After sign-in, a mismatch offers an account switch or correction. Discard unverified submissions. A state-changing POST confirms interest. |
| **Request received** | Show the same acknowledgement for an eligible, suppressed, denied or duplicate submission. Do not offer a public account lookup or reveal queue position. An authenticated outage returns a retryable service error. It must not imply the request was recorded. |
| **Owner preview** | Show verified recipient, request time, reason for eligibility, policy version and current state. Display the full address only in this protected view when needed for a decision. “Admin” means the sole owner. Deny records a reason and closes the request. Stale, double or conflicting decisions return the recorded outcome or require refresh. |
| **Send invite** | For an approved request, the owner confirms the displayed address. For a direct invitation, the owner enters and confirms an address without fabricating a visitor request. Both paths enforce holds, bans, existing membership, duplicate checks, cooldown and mail budget before queuing one send. An outstanding denial requires an explicit, audited owner decision to reopen. |
| **Redeem** | Invitation email contains a fixed admission URL and a separate one-time code. Opening the URL, including by a mail scanner, has no side effect. The email explains the ten-minute window. The matching signed-in recipient can see the UTC deadline once the send time is confirmed. The signed-in recipient must submit the code by POST before that deadline. A successful redemption shows “Setting up access” until authorization is ready, then offers the workspace link. |

### System 1 and repeat abuse

Proposed first version: a bounded, versioned rules classifier over verified account identity, prior application state and server-observed behavior. No free-text justification or external model is needed to request access. Record outcome, reason code, timestamp, policy version and event ID. Evaluate in this order:

| Condition | Disposition | Owner preview and enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| Identity missing or invalid form | Reject or request correction | No preview, send or strike. Anonymous traffic gets a separate short-lived rate limit. |
| Active ban on the verified actor | Suppress | No preview, send or new strike. Retries cannot extend the ban. |
| Typed address differs from the verified account | Reject the submission and offer correction | No request for the typed recipient. An isolated mismatch earns zero strikes. Evaluate only the verified actor's post-warning pattern under the rule below. |
| Replay of an event, existing pending request or invitation, existing member, or denial cooldown | Deduplicate | Coalesce equivalent attempts. No additional review row, send or strike. A new idempotency key does not make the same interest new. |
| Objective abuse attributable to the verified actor | Spam | Withhold from preview. Record a qualifying strike once per distinct abuse window. |
| Inconclusive evidence | Hold | No approval row or strike. Re-evaluate when reliable evidence becomes available or the actor completes a bounded re-verification. Expire unresolved holds. |
| Verified self-request, checks healthy and passed | Valid | Create or return one owner-reviewable request. No automatic approval or delivery. |

**Approved local pilot ban rule.** A qualifying event is a verified actor repeatedly attempting submissions for distinct third-party addresses after an explicit self-only warning. Require at least three distinct targets in a ten-minute window after that warning. Never persist those targets as applicants or strike them. Count at most one strike per actor per non-overlapping ten-minute window. Three qualifying windows in seven days cause a seven-day invitation ban. Evaluate the strike and ban atomically before publishing any review item. A ban withdraws that actor's pending preview items, cancels queued sends and invalidates outstanding codes for known matching identities. Check the ban again at redemption. Existing workspace membership has a separate owner revocation control. IP address, shared networks, typos, denials, ambiguous results and repeated self-requests alone earn zero strikes. Apply independent volume limits to correct self-requests even when they are duplicates. If strike storage is unavailable, defer admission without penalizing anyone.

Persist a mismatch counter only after verified sign-in. The first mismatch offers correction and starts no strike. Subsequent post-warning attempts matching the abuse rule generate **actor-only** evidence before the mismatch is discarded. Bound target correlation to short-lived keyed fingerprints. An existing pending self-request must not mask this separate abuse signal.

Allow one authenticated appeal per ban through a separate moderation view. Include a reference and reason codes there. An appeal cannot place the original application in the approval queue. The owner can clear an erroneous strike or ban with an audited reason, then rescreen. An expired ban permits a fresh request. Reprocessing old events cannot reinstate it.

### Identity and authorization

- Keep the [public intro](../../week2/landing.py) static, including its fixed workspace redirect. The separate admission service verifies Google OpenID Connect identity for applicants and invitees before IAP would admit them to the workspace. Use a fixed callback, authorization-code flow, state, nonce, PKCE, secure HTTP-only session cookies, origin/CSRF checks and server-side verification of issuer, audience, expiry and verified email. Protect the sender and approval endpoints independently with the owner's verified principal. [Google OpenID Connect](https://developers.google.com/identity/openid-connect/openid-connect)
- Keys for abuse, membership and ownership derive from verified provider and stable subject. Use conservative address matching for the first invitation, then bind to the subject. Trim surrounding whitespace and normalize the domain. Never infer identity by stripping dots, plus tags, or matching a local part across domains. For a self-request, remember the verified subject as well as the approved delivery address. Require both on redemption. For a direct invite, first redemption proves both mailbox possession and the matching Google account. Address change or reassignment needs owner reconciliation.
- Keep the [IAP verifier](../../week2/iap.py) and [principal-bound sessions](../../week2/session_boundary.py). A narrowly scoped worker reconciles only redeemed memberships with the workspace's service-level IAP access policy, using version checks and preserving unrelated bindings. It has no role on the public intro and cannot make the app publicly invokable. Separate identity verification from the current static email allowlist. Admit a user only when the database records an active membership **and** the verified IAP identity belongs to its bound Google account. Prove how the Google OIDC subject maps to the signed IAP subject for this provider using fixtures and an approved staging check. Fail closed if that binding cannot be established. [IAP signed identity](https://docs.cloud.google.com/iap/docs/signed-headers-howto)
- On revocation, deny at the application first, invalidate sessions and queued deliveries, then reconcile removal at IAP. Recheck authorization on API calls, stream reconnects and long-lived sessions within the revocation target. The current owner remains the sole owner until the future owner confirms the complete account privately, proves control and an explicit transfer is approved. No invite carries an owner role.

### Invitation lifecycle and failure handling

| Record | States and recovery |
|---|---|
| Interest request | Screened → pending owner review → approved or denied. Spam and held requests stay outside preview. A ban, expiry or changed eligibility can close a pending/approved request before send. |
| Invitation | Queued → sent → redeemed, expired or revoked. Worker outcomes also include known delivery failure and delivery unknown. Exactly one usable invitation per canonical recipient. An owner-initiated replacement invalidates the old code in the same transaction. |
| Membership | Redeemed → provisioning → active → revoked. Partial IAP updates remain pending and are reconciled. A failed or stale worker must never resurrect a revoked membership. A retry of a successful redemption can show the same account its provisioning status. |

**Per-recipient deadline.** For each logical invitation email, persist the original mail provider acceptance time as `sent_at` in UTC and set `expires_at = sent_at + 600 seconds`. For local tests the mail sink supplies that timestamp. Use a trusted server clock inside the atomic redemption decision. The eligible interval is `sent_at <= now < expires_at`. At the deadline or later, an unused code is expired immediately, even if a cleanup worker has not run. Opening the email, starting Google sign-in, retries, and delivery reconciliation cannot extend the window. If delivery is uncertain, defer redemption until the original send time can be established. A confirmed timely redemption may finish provisioning after the deadline. Expiry by itself triggers no resend, grant, strike or ban. Only an explicit owner replacement, subject to the separate cooldown, can start a fresh window for that recipient.

Use a durable datastore with transactions, conditional writes and uniqueness across instances. Commit the invite and its delivery job together. Check the current hold, ban, cancellation and revocation immediately before dispatch. Never redispatch an expired invitation. Store a digest of a cryptographically random code of at least 128 bits. Limit attempts by actor and invitation. Keep the mail worker's dispatch payload encrypted with restricted access until delivery reconciliation or expiry. Codes stay out of URLs, logs, analytics and Sheets. After an ambiguous provider timeout, retain the same delivery ID, reconcile with the provider or require owner resolution. Repeating the owner click must never blindly send a second message. Redeem and consume atomically. A forwarded code, email preview or signed-in wrong account cannot consume it.

### Google Sheets, privacy and load

- Proposed sheet columns: stable request/event reference, masked email, UTC time, source (interest or owner-direct), classifier decision/reason/version, owner decision, delivery/redemption state and repeat count. Limit file access to the owner and a dedicated reporting identity. Show suppressed traffic as counts with bounded detail. The owner can see full recipient details only in the protected review UI. No raw tokens, identity assertions, IPs or invitation links in the sheet.
- Export through a separate durable queue. Use stable event IDs and deterministic row assignments so retrying an acknowledged-or-unknown write updates the same row. Handle quota errors with bounded batches, backoff and reconciliation. Write cells as literal values. Sheet edits or deletions cannot approve, email, ban or grant. Exports can be rebuilt from retained events. Surface export lag and alert on a backlog. [Sheets API limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/sheets/api/limits) · [Literal value input](https://developers.google.com/workspace/sheets/api/reference/rest/v4/ValueInputOption)
- Bound per-actor submissions, per-recipient mail, per-network intake and total dispatch independently. Give the owner a global send pause and daily budget. Shared networks can slow intake but cannot ban a person. Protect the owner review queue from floods by many distinct accounts. Surface queue age and saturation; return a retryable response when durable intake is full. Monitor p95 intake/decision latency, dedupe rate, false blocks, ban reversals, queued mail, delivery-unknown events, IAM propagation and Sheets lag.
- **Local pilot defaults.** Each sent email has the ten-minute redemption window above. The replacement cooldown is 24 hours, denied-request cooldown seven days, uncertain hold 24 hours, and one appeal per ban. Use 30 days as a proposed ceiling for identifiable request history after closure and reporting rows, with expiry of fingerprints at the end of their counting window. Keep minimal membership data while access is active. Document deletion from the authoritative store, report and backups, and sanitize audit events before writing them to the existing retention-locked audit bucket. Set actual budgets, rates and production retention with the owner.

## Acceptance evidence for implementation

Run these as synthetic, offline checks on Python 3.13 with a fake clock, separate identities, at least two app instances sharing storage, a mail sink, and Sheets/IAM test doubles. Retain redacted results by source revision and classifier version.

| Check | Required observation |
|---|---|
| Both entry paths | Valid interest appears once for the owner. Deny sends zero emails; approve alone sends zero; owner send creates one job. Owner-direct follows the same delivery controls. Anonymous applicants and ordinary members cannot directly invoke owner operations. |
| Identity and redemption | Test typed/account mismatch, forged or wrong-audience claims, forwarded/expired/revoked codes, concurrent redemptions and a retried success. One intended account receives one user membership. Verify cross-user data isolation and both IAP/application denial during incomplete provisioning. |
| Per-email expiry | With a fake clock, exercise separate invitations just before 600 seconds, exactly at 600 seconds and just after. Only the first can be redeemed. Stagger sends for two recipients and verify independent deadlines across instances and a restart. A late delivery acknowledgement retains the original deadline. Expiry has zero grant, resend or strike side effects. |
| Classification and bans | Freeze fixtures for legitimate, spam, uncertain and duplicate traffic. Record each disposition, false-block and spam-to-preview rates with sample counts, separately from ordinary duplicates. All reference spam and held fixtures stay outside preview. Ordinary retries and users sharing a network earn zero strikes. Verify third-window ban, ban expiry, appeal, owner lift and recovery from a false positive. Synthetic results do not establish production accuracy. |
| Concurrency and delivery | Race 100 equivalent submissions and 20 send clicks across two instances including a restart. Require one preview row and one logical delivery. Exercise approval versus deny, send versus revoke, stale worker, provider timeout after acceptance and IAM retry after revoke. No duplicate automatic send or access resurrection. |
| Reporting and load | Pilot with 1,000 distinct synthetic accounts over ten minutes and a separate same-recipient burst. Record throughput, latency, backlog, mail count, quota usage and projected cost. Simulate Sheets 429s, outage, retry after partial success and manual cell edits. Reconcile rows exactly once per event with zero mail or membership changes from reporting. |
| Access hold and rollback | With the hold enabled, server-side entrypoints and workers send zero emails and issue zero new grants, including work queued before the hold. Scope rollback to invitations and non-owner admission, preserve the owner's tested access, and retain delivery history for recovery. |

## Policy conformance

The [CRA2 contributor guidance](../../CLAUDE.md) and [existing access contract](../cra-versions/spec.md) apply. This classifier governs invitation requests only. Masked active-user presence stays inside the signed-in workspace. The public start screen contains no identities. Use synthetic accounts and no real email recipients or shared sheet during development. Independent labeling remains on hold. Rollout requires an explicit owner decision after testing.

## Flagged concerns for review

1. **Owner identity.** Full address, proof of control, recovery path and a separate owner-transfer decision remain outstanding. They are unnecessary for drafting or synthetic tests.
2. **Moderation policy.** The ban threshold and invitation acceptance window are settled for local testing. Before launch, review measured false-block and spam-to-preview rates, appeal handling, production cooldowns and any additional qualifying evidence.
3. **Launch envelope.** Confirm recipients per day, burst size, owner review capacity, spend ceiling, p95 latency, revocation bound, delivery SLA and export-lag target. A 1,000-account fixture measures a stated workload only.
4. **Operational integrations.** Select the transactional store and mail provider; verify idempotency/reconciliation, authorized sender, scoped IAM permissions and policy size, OIDC-to-IAP subject binding, external-account OAuth availability and required egress. Confirm private Sheet ownership, minimal fields and retention before enabling integrations.
